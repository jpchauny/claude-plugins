# Drift management

**Audience:** The people who run a project's flow from specs to code; whoever builds the skills of the plugin `drift-management`

**Scope:** A drift found while defining acceptance in layer 3 or implementing in layer 4, sent back to where it is fixed: layer 1, or the plugin of the derived-norms generator that made a defect of a derived-norms generator. What each layer holds is in [layers.md](../../development-methodology/specs/layers.md)

The module `drift-management` of the app Indra Software Factory, listed in [app.md](../../../specs/app.md).

## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Drift | A norm or a derived norm of layer 2 that defining acceptance in layer 3 or implementing in layer 4 shows to be wrong, missing or contradictory ([LAY-5](../../development-methodology/specs/layers.md#rules)). |
| Defect of a derived-norms generator | A drift that is a derived norm which does not follow from the norms it cites, while those norms are right: the exception of [LAY-5](../../development-methodology/specs/layers.md#rules). It is fixed in the plugin of its derived-norms generator, not in layer 1. |

The term run is that of [flow.md](../../development-methodology/specs/flow.md#terms). The terms norm, norm hash, derived norm, derived norm ID and derived norm fingerprint are those of [layers.md](../../development-methodology/specs/layers.md#terms); the terms derived-norms generator, status file and list of derived norms are those of [norms-management](../../norms-management/specs/module.md#terms); the terms acceptance unit and scenario are those of [acceptance-management](../../acceptance-management/specs/module.md#terms); the term marketplace repository is that of [define.md](../../acceptance-management/use-cases/define/specs/define.md#terms).

## Report a drift

- **ORC-14.** When the step of [acceptance-management](../../acceptance-management/specs/module.md) or the step of [code-implementation](../../code-implementation/specs/module.md) finds a drift, it does not work around it, and opens one issue for it, once the person who runs its use case confirms the text of the issue ([LAY-5](../../development-methodology/specs/layers.md#rules)). The issue goes to (complete list):
  - for a defect of a derived-norms generator: the marketplace repository: that of the plugin that `generator` of the status file of the type of the derived norm names, at the commit of layer 2 that the step read ([NRM-5](../../norms-management/specs/module.md#derive-the-derived-norms)), found as [ACU-79](../../acceptance-management/use-cases/define/specs/define.md#define-the-acceptance-areas-and-acceptance-units) says. The issue names the derived norm by its derived norm ID and derived norm fingerprint, its type, the norms it cites, each with its norm hash, and the `plugin`, `version` and `marketplace_commit` of `generator` of that status file;
  - for every other drift: the repository of layer 1.

  Exceptions, in which the step opens no issue for the drift (complete list):
  - the person who runs its use case does not say yes to the text of the issue;
  - the step cannot find where the issue goes, and so asks no question of it: for a drift sent to layer 1, when `project.json` has not exactly one entry of layer 1 ([ACU-68](../../acceptance-management/use-cases/define/specs/define.md#define-the-acceptance-areas-and-acceptance-units) for the step of acceptance-management, this rule for the step of code-implementation); for a defect of a derived-norms generator, when the derived norm cannot be looked up in the list of derived norms of its type at the commit of layer 2 that the step read, when that commit holds no status file of the type with a `generator`, or when no installed marketplace on GitHub holds the plugin that `generator` names ([ACU-79](../../acceptance-management/use-cases/define/specs/define.md#define-the-acceptance-areas-and-acceptance-units), [IMP-10](../../code-implementation/use-cases/implement/specs/implement.md#implement-a-task));
  - `gh issue create` fails for the issue ([ACU-68](../../acceptance-management/use-cases/define/specs/define.md#define-the-acceptance-areas-and-acceptance-units), [ACU-79](../../acceptance-management/use-cases/define/specs/define.md#define-the-acceptance-areas-and-acceptance-units), [IMP-10](../../code-implementation/use-cases/implement/specs/implement.md#implement-a-task), and this rule for a drift sent to layer 1 by the step of code-implementation).

  In the last two the step says why no issue is opened. The message is (complete list):
  - for the step of acceptance-management: the one that [ACU-68](../../acceptance-management/use-cases/define/specs/define.md#define-the-acceptance-areas-and-acceptance-units) gives for a drift sent to layer 1, and the one that [ACU-79](../../acceptance-management/use-cases/define/specs/define.md#define-the-acceptance-areas-and-acceptance-units) gives for a defect of a derived-norms generator;
  - for the step of code-implementation, for a defect of a derived-norms generator: the one that [IMP-10](../../code-implementation/use-cases/implement/specs/implement.md#implement-a-task) gives;
  - for the step of code-implementation, for a drift sent to layer 1: the one that [ACU-68](../../acceptance-management/use-cases/define/specs/define.md#define-the-acceptance-areas-and-acceptance-units) gives, with `code-implementation:implement:` where ACU-68 says `acceptance-management:define:`, and otherwise byte for byte once filled. So it is `code-implementation:implement: project.json has not exactly one entry of layer 1, the specs repository; no issue is opened` when `project.json` has not exactly one entry of layer 1, and `code-implementation:implement: cannot open the issue in <owner>/<name>` when `gh issue create` fails, where `<owner>/<name>` is that of the repository of layer 1 in `project.json`. ORC-14 gives these two messages because IMP-10 gives none for a drift sent to layer 1.

  In each of the three the step still does not work around the drift, and the run still ends for the norms and the derived norms it touches, as below; the drift then reaches whoever fixes it only through the person who runs the use case.

  The run ends there for the norms and the derived norms the drift touches. How the fix reaches layer 2 (complete list):
  - for a drift sent to layer 1: a new run starts from the commit of layer 1 that fixes it, and its derived-norms generators derive again what that change of the norms calls for ([NRM-7](../../norms-management/specs/module.md#derive-the-derived-norms));
  - for a defect of a derived-norms generator: the fix is a new version of the plugin, and layer 2 changes at the next run, which uses it ([LAY-6](../../development-methodology/specs/layers.md#rules)) and derives again the output of its type, also when no norm changed ([NRM-7](../../norms-management/specs/module.md#derive-the-derived-norms)). The fixed derived norm then gets another derived norm fingerprint, or is gone ([LAY-12](../../development-methodology/specs/layers.md#rules) item 2), so the run goes on with layers 3 and 4 ([ORC-10](../../norms-management/specs/module.md#regenerate-the-norms)), which update the acceptance units and the code that cite it. Until that run the list of derived norms keeps it with the derived norm fingerprint that the issue names: layer 3 builds no scenario from what it adds ([ACU-79](../../acceptance-management/use-cases/define/specs/define.md#define-the-acceptance-areas-and-acceptance-units)), and layer 4 builds no code from it ([IMP-10](../../code-implementation/use-cases/implement/specs/implement.md#implement-a-task)).

  Rationale: an issue in the repository where the drift is fixed reaches the people who fix it; why that is layer 1, or the plugin for a defect of a derived-norms generator, is the reason of [LAY-5](../../development-methodology/specs/layers.md#rules). The derived norm ID, the derived norm fingerprint and the version of the generator that the issue names let whoever fixes it find the derived norm and the version that made it, and tell from the next run whether it changed. The person's yes keeps an issue from being opened from a misreading of the step. When no issue can be opened, ending the run for what the drift touches still keeps layers 3 and 4 from building on it, and saying why lets the person report it by hand.
